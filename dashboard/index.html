Are Maharashtra's farmers actually getting the government's minimum price?

Every month, 349 regulated markets (APMCs) across Maharashtra report the price farmers received for their produce. The government sets a Minimum Support Price (MSP) for staple crops as a price floor. This analysis checks 20,707 real transactions against that floor — and finds it's being missed more often, and more expensively, than you'd expect.

What this means for a stakeholder

If I were handing this to a state agriculture department or an agri-procurement team, here's the one-paragraph takeaway.

Roughly 1 in 4 MSP-eligible sales across Maharashtra's mandis happened below the government's price floor, concentrated in a small number of districts (Buldhana, Jalgaon, Akola) and commodities (jowar, maize, sunflower). The pattern is strongest right after harvest, when arrivals spike and prices soften — exactly when farmers have the least leverage to hold out for a better price. The clearest lever is targeted procurement or storage support in the worst-performing districts during October–November, rather than a blanket statewide policy.

Does oversupply drive prices below MSP?

Transactions bucketed by arrival volume (quintals reaching the market that month).

Low-volume markets (under 100 quintals) are violated 30.6% of the time versus 19.4% for high-volume markets (2,000+ quintals) — the opposite of the "oversupply crashes the price" story. This suggests it's small, thin markets with little buyer competition — not big harvest gluts — that are the real driver, which changes where an intervention should target: market infrastructure and buyer access in small mandis, not just harvest-time storage.

Data, method & honest limitations

Source data

Maharashtra APMC monthly commodity price bulletins (CMO), 62,429 records, 349 markets, 352 raw commodity labels across 2014–2016, originally published via data.gov.in / state Cooperation, Marketing & Textiles department open data. Government Minimum Support Price (MSP) reference table, 2012–2016, merged in on commodity + year.

Cleaning

352 raw commodity labels collapsed to 202 after normalizing case/whitespace. 296 rows with a logically impossible min > max price and 216 rows with a zero/negative price were flagged and excluded from analysis (0.78% of records) rather than silently dropped. Full before/after counts are in docs/data_quality_report.md in the project repo.

MSP scope

MSP only exists for 19 of the 202 commodities in this data — the staple/policy crops (cereals, pulses, oilseeds, cotton, sugarcane). It does not apply to most fruits and vegetables, so the compliance analysis is intentionally scoped to those 19 commodities (20,707 of the 61,944 valid transactions).

The coconut flag

Coconut's average recorded arrival volume (~98,090 quintals/transaction) is 2nd-highest of any commodity and implausible next to comparable crops. Its shortfall (₹104.6 Cr, 42 transactions) is reported separately and excluded from every headline number in this dashboard. This is disclosed, not hidden — a real analyst decision, not a data science trick.

What this is not

This is a descriptive, historical analysis (2014–2016, Maharashtra only) — it shows where and when MSP violations concentrated, not why in a causal sense. "Oversupply" and "thin markets" are read from correlations in the arrival-volume data, not a controlled test.

Stack

Python (pandas) for cleaning, SQLite + SQL for the business-question queries (window functions, ranking, aggregation), this dashboard for presentation. Full pipeline — cleaning script, SQL file, EDA script — ships with the project repo.
